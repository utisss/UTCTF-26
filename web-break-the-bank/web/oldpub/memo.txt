FIRST NATIONAL SAVINGS BANK
INTERNAL MEMORANDUM

TO:      Application Development Team
FROM:    Harold J. Whitmore, President & Chief Executive Officer
         cc: Margaret L. Osei, VP of Information Technology
             Dennis R. Kaczmarek, Director of Application Development
DATE:    November 3, 1997
RE:      Technology Modernization Initiative — Status & Priorities
CLASSIFICATION: INTERNAL USE ONLY

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Team,

I want to take a moment to address the progress we have made — and the
work that remains — as we continue our effort to modernize the bank's
core technology infrastructure.

As many of you are aware, FNSB has operated on a foundation of legacy
systems built over several decades. Our primary core banking platform
dates to 1974, and while it has served us reliably, the constraints it
places on our ability to deliver new products and services to our
customers are becoming increasingly difficult to justify. The board has
reaffirmed its commitment to the modernization initiative, and it falls
to this team to execute on that vision responsibly.


I. CURRENT STATE

The migration of our customer-facing Internet Banking portal from the
mainframe-hosted batch-processing model to our new Java-based
application server (MAINFRAME-B, running the iBank v2.1.4 platform) has
been an important first step. The team should be proud of what has been
accomplished in a short period of time. Online account access, balance
inquiry, and fund transfers are now live, and customer adoption has
exceeded our projections.

That said, we are keenly aware that this is a first iteration. The
current stack is a bridge between old and new, not a destination. Much
of the underlying data model still mirrors the flat-file structures of
the legacy system, and our session management and authentication
infrastructure carries forward assumptions that made sense in a closed
mainframe environment but require rethinking for a public-facing
internet application.


II. MODERNIZATION CHALLENGES

Moving a bank of our size and history to a modern application stack is
not a straightforward undertaking, and I want to be direct with the
team about where I see the friction.

Data migration remains our most significant technical risk. The core
account ledger, loan origination records, and transaction history span
over twenty years of proprietary data formats. We do not yet have a
complete and validated migration path for the oldest records, and until
we do, the legacy system cannot be decommissioned. The team should
continue to treat the mainframe as the system of record and the new
platform as a parallel layer, not a replacement.

Integration complexity is a close second. Our ACH gateway, wire
transfer processing, and the FDIC reporting pipeline all rely on
interfaces written specifically for the legacy system. Rewriting or
wrapping these interfaces for the new stack without introducing
settlement errors or regulatory reporting gaps will require careful
coordination with both the operations team and our external partners at
the Federal Reserve and correspondent banks.

Organizational readiness also deserves an honest assessment. Many of
our branch staff and back-office personnel have worked with the legacy
system for their entire careers at FNSB. Training, change management,
and support must be built into the project plan as first-class concerns,
not afterthoughts.


III. Y2K COMPLIANCE

The team is already well aware of this, but I want it stated plainly in
writing: Y2K remediation takes priority over all feature development
between now and January 1, 2000. At 71% completion we are on track, but
there is no margin for schedule slippage. Dennis, please ensure the
weekly status reports to the board continue without interruption.


IV. SECURITY PRACTICES

As we expand our public internet footprint, I want to direct the team's
attention to our internal security practices. The move to a networked,
browser-accessible platform meaningfully increases our attack surface
relative to the closed mainframe environment we are accustomed to. I am
asking the team to invest more deliberately in security review as part
of the development process — not as a checkpoint at the end of a
project, but as an ongoing discipline throughout.

Margaret has identified several areas for immediate attention, including
a review of how we manage authentication credentials in the new
platform. On that note — and I will keep this brief as the specific
remediation steps are documented separately — the team should ensure
that any development or convenience credentials that were introduced
during the initial build and testing phases are reviewed and retired
before the platform is considered production-ready. Leaving accounts
provisioned for testing purposes active in a production environment is
not consistent with our obligations to our customers or our regulators.
Please treat this as an action item with near-term resolution.

More broadly, I am asking Dennis to establish a recurring internal
security review cycle for the iBank platform and to report findings to
Margaret's team on a quarterly basis going forward.


V. LOOKING AHEAD

Our target remains a fully modernized, integrated core banking platform
by the end of fiscal year 2001. That is an ambitious timeline given the
scope of the work, and it will require sustained focus from this team.
I am confident in your ability to deliver.

Thank you for your continued dedication to this institution and to the
customers who depend on us.

Harold J. Whitmore
President & Chief Executive Officer
First National Savings Bank
