HINT 1 — What is an LCG and why is it weak?
- An LCG is a formula that generates "random" numbers one after another
- The formula is: next_number = (a * current_number + c) % m
- The problem is that a and c are SECRET but fixed — they never change
- This means if you see enough outputs, you can figure out what a and c are
- Once you know a and c, you can predict every future number the generator produces
- Google "LCG attack" or "cracking linear congruential generator" for more info

HINT 2 — How do you find the secret a?
- You have 4 outputs. Call the first three: x1, x2, x3
- You know x2 was made from x1, and x3 was made from x2
- If you subtract those two relationships the c cancels out, leaving only a
- The formula to get a is:
    a = (x3 - x2) * modular_inverse(x2 - x1, m) % m
- modular_inverse just means "division in modular math" — use sympy for it:
    from sympy import mod_inverse
    a = (x3 - x2) * mod_inverse(x2 - x1, m) % m

HINT 3 — How do you find c and predict the next number?
- Once you have a, getting c is simple:
    c = (x2 - a * x1) % m
- Now you can predict output_5 (the one after the 4 you were given):
    output_5 = (a * output_4 + c) % m
- output_5 is the decryption key!
